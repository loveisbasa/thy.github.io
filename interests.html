Haoyue Tang–Research Interests

My research aims at exploring the fundamental limits, designing efficient algorithms and prototyping for decision making in stochastic networks and stochastic systems.

Current Interests

  • Efficient Long Sequence Modeling through Software-Hardware Co-Design

  • Foundations for Diffusion and Flow Matching Models

  • Applications of Machine Learning Methods in Healthcare and Networking

Research Projects

  • Classifier-Free Guidance in Diffusion Models: Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the standard mechanism for conditional sampling in diffusion models, and essentially every deployed text-to-image system depends on it. The goal of this project is to understand the mechanism of CFG, develop efficient ways to implement CFG in inverse problems, and improve the CFG method in general.

    • Supported by NAIRR260411 (GPU Resources for Teaching Information-Theoretic Deep Generative Models, ECE 693B, University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa) and NSF ELE260074: Diagnosing and Improving Conditional Score Estimation for Classifier-Free Guidance.

    • H. Tang, T. Xie, A. Feng, H. Wang, C. Zhang and Y. Bai, “Solving General Inverse Problems via Posterior Sampling: A Policy Gradient Viewpoint,” in Proceedings of The 27th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Statistics (AISTATS 2024). Poster

  • Efficient Long Sequence Modeling through Software-Hardware Co-Design: Efficient long sequence modeling is important for applications such as recommendation systems and drug discovery. In recommendation systems, a sequence of past user interactions is summarized into a few tokens and fed into deep neural networks for user-content interaction prediction. Utilizing attention for sequence modeling faces two challenges: (1) summarizing the full long behaviour sequence is GPU-memory costly; (2) over long sequences attention weights are over-allocated to irrelevant events, reducing accuracy. Our research proposes a semantic-aware, hierarchical sequence pruning mechanism developed jointly with the attention kernel that executes it, enabling a synergistic software-hardware co-design approach for efficient and accurate long sequence modeling.

  • Online Learning for Network Optimization: How to optimally manage information freshness when channel statistics are unknown? Using Age of Information (AoI) as a metric, we present a stochastic approximation algorithm that learns the optimum sampling policy almost surely with minimax order optimum regret. Supported by NSF-AI Institute Athena.

    • H. Tang, Y. Sun, and L. Tassiulas, “Sampling of the Wiener Process for Remote Estimation Over a Channel with Unknown Delay Statistics,” IEEE/ACM Transactions on Networking, vol. 32, no. 3, pp. 1920-1935, Jun. 2024.

    • H. Tang, Y. Chen, J. Wang, P. Yang and L. Tassiulas, “Age Optimal Sampling Under Unknown Delay Statistics,” IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, vol. 69, no. 2, pp. 1295-1314, Feb. 2023.

    • H. Tang, Y. Sun, and L. Tassiulas, “Sampling of the Wiener Process for Remote Estimation Over a Channel with Unknown Delay Statistics,” in ACM Mobihoc 2022, pp. 51–60.

    • H. Tang, Y. Chen, J. Sun, J. Wang and J. Song, “Sending Timely Status Updates through Channel with Random Delay via Online Learning,” IEEE INFOCOM 2022, pp. 1819-1827.

  • Cross-Layer Scheduling for Data Freshness Optimization: Jointly optimizing data sampling, power control and scheduling to minimize Age of Information in multi-user wireless networks with energy constraints and time-varying channels. We propose the first optimal multi-user scheduling algorithm in AoI literature.

    • H. Tang, J. Wang, L. Song and J. Song, “Minimizing Age of Information With Power Constraints: Multi-User Opportunistic Scheduling in Multi-State Time-Varying Channels,” IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications, vol. 38, no. 5, pp. 854-868, May 2020.

    • H. Tang, J. Wang, Z. Tang and J. Song, “Scheduling to Minimize Age of Synchronization in Wireless Broadcast Networks With Random Updates,” IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications, vol. 19, no. 6, pp. 4023-4037, June 2020.

  • Signal Processing for mmWave Channel Estimation: Off-grid sparse Bayesian learning algorithm for uplink mmWave massive MIMO channel estimation, exploiting spatial sparsity to identify scatterer angles and gains with lower MSE than conventional methods.

    • H. Tang, J. Wang and L. He, “Off-Grid Sparse Bayesian Learning-Based Channel Estimation for MmWave Massive MIMO Uplink,” IEEE Wireless Communications Letters, vol. 8, no. 1, pp. 45-48, Feb. 2019.

  • Domain Adaptation and Out-of-Distribution Generation via Causal Inference: We propose a Causal Semantic Generative model (CSG) that separates semantic and variation factors via causal reasoning and variational Bayesian inference, achieving improved OOD prediction from a single training domain. Work done during internship at Microsoft Research.

    • C. Liu, X. Sun, J. Wang, H. Tang, T. Li, T. Qin, W. Chen, and T. Liu, “Learning Causal Semantic Representation for Out-of-Distribution Prediction,” NeurIPS 2021: 6155-6170.
