Outline

Language models are the core of modern AI systems. In this course, students build one from scratch. Following Stanford CS336: Language Modeling from Scratch and the previous offering of ECE405 by Prof. Igor Molybog, we go through the full pipeline of a language model: tokenization, Transformer architecture and training, GPU systems and kernels, distributed training, scaling laws, data collection and filtering, inference, evaluation, and post-training (supervised fine-tuning, preference alignment, and reinforcement learning for reasoning). Aim: for students to gain the engineering ability to reproduce results from AI papers. The course is implementation-heavy: four large coding assignments run on GPU clusters.

Prerequisites

Linear algebra, calculus, probability & statistics, and basic machine learning. A solid coding background is required: Python, PyTorch, bash, and git.

Grading

  • Assignment 1 (Basics): 25%

  • Assignment 2 (Systems & Scaling): 25%

  • Assignment 3 (Data): 25%

  • Assignment 4 (Alignment & Reasoning RL): 25% No exams. Letter grades follow the UH Mānoa plus/minus scale (see the syllabus).

Course Policies

  • Late Days: 5 late days for the semester, at most 3 per assignment. Each late day extends a deadline by 24 hours.

  • Regrades: Within 3 days after grades are released, for objective grading errors only.

  • Collaboration: Study groups are allowed, but each student writes and submits their own code and write-up. List study-group members on your submission.

  • AI Tools: LLMs may be used for low-level programming questions and high-level conceptual questions, but not to solve assignment problems directly. Please turn off AI autocomplete in your IDE. Do not consult third-party implementations unless a handout says so.

  • Academic Integrity: Students are expected to adhere to the UH Mānoa Student Conduct Code.

  • Accessibility (KOKUA): Students needing accommodations should contact the KOKUA Program.
